How to import Hyperliquid into Koinly

Updated October 2026. Applies to perps, spot and funding on Hyperliquid mainnet.

Why the native sync is not enough

Koinly can read a Hyperliquid address directly, and for simple spot activity that works. Perps are where it breaks. Koinly's own forum has threads about missing purchase history on significant transactions, staked HYPE not appearing, and perp closes modelled as disposals of tokens you never held. Three things cause this:

  • A perp close is not a sale of an asset. You never owned ETH; you owned a contract. The taxable number is the realized PnL on the close, not "sold 1 ETH at 3,100".
  • Funding settles every hour. One open position produces up to 8,760 tiny USDC movements a year. Imported one by one they clutter the ledger and often get matched to the wrong thing.
  • Perp ↔ spot and vault moves are internal. Moving USDC between your own perp and spot balances is not a transfer in or out of the exchange, but a raw import treats it as one.

Step 1: build a Koinly-ready CSV

Open the exporter, paste your wallet address, choose the tax year and click Build export. The page calls Hyperliquid's public API from your browser and produces three files. The one you need here is …-koinly.csv. It uses Koinly's universal format with these conventions:

EventKoinly row
Perp close with profitReceived USDC, label realized gain
Perp close with lossSent USDC, label realized gain. Koinly books a sent realized-gain row as a loss.
Trading feeSent USDC, label cost
FundingNet per UTC day per asset, label realized gain (or income / cost, your choice)
Deposit / withdrawalPlain transfer row, no label. Koinly matches it to your Arbitrum wallet.
Spot tradeUSDC ↔ token swap
Perp ↔ spot, vaults, sub-accountsNot in the file. They are internal.

Step 2: create the wallet in Koinly

In Koinly go to Wallets → Add wallet, search for Hyperliquid and pick Import from file instead of the API/address sync. If you already have a synced Hyperliquid wallet with wrong numbers, create a second wallet named "Hyperliquid (CSV)" and later delete the synced one, so you can compare both before deciding.

Step 3: upload and check the totals

Upload the CSV. Then open the wallet's transactions, filter by label realized gain, and compare the sum to the TOTAL realized PnL line in summary.txt. They should match to the cent. If funding looks doubled, you probably also have the synced wallet active; remove it.

Matching deposits and withdrawals

Hyperliquid deposits arrive from Arbitrum (USDC bridge). Koinly matches the "Received 5,000 USDC" row in the Hyperliquid wallet with the "Sent 5,000 USDC" in your Arbitrum wallet automatically when the timestamps are within a few hours. If it shows them as separate deposit and withdrawal, select both and use Merge.

Common questions

Should funding be "realized gain" or "income"?

Both approaches exist. Treating net funding as part of trading PnL (realized gain) is the most common with accountants who treat perps as derivatives. Treating received funding as income and paid funding as cost is used where interest-like flows are taxed separately. The exporter supports both; ask your accountant which one your country expects.

What about liquidations?

A liquidation produces a fill with the realized loss in closedPnl, so it is already in the file as a realized gain row with a sent amount.

Spot fees charged in the token (for example HYPE)?

They are valued at the fill price and reported in USDC, which is what Koinly needs for the fee column.

Does it cover sub-accounts?

Export each sub-account address separately. Transfers between them are internal and are excluded.
