Hyperliquid funding payments and taxes

Updated October 2026.

What funding is

A perpetual contract has no expiry, so the exchange keeps its price near the spot price with funding: every hour, longs pay shorts or shorts pay longs a small amount proportional to position size and the funding rate. On Hyperliquid funding settles hourly, and the premium is sampled several times inside each hour. A position held for a year is 8,760 settlements. Each one is a real USDC movement in your account.

Why it matters for tax

Funding is money received or paid, separate from the profit or loss when you close the position. If you only report closed PnL, you under- or over-report by the net funding. For an active account the net funding can be larger than the trading result itself. Three practical problems:

  • Volume. Thousands of rows per position. Tax tools slow down and accountants charge by the hour.
  • Sign. Received funding and paid funding are both "funding" in the API; the sign of the USDC delta tells them apart.
  • Classification. Some jurisdictions fold funding into derivative PnL; some treat received funding like interest income and paid funding like a cost.

The two ways to report it

ApproachHow the rows lookWhen it is used
Part of trading PnLNet funding per day as a realized gain (positive) or realized loss (negative)Countries that treat perps as derivatives and tax the overall result
Income and costReceived funding as income, paid funding as costWhere interest-like inflows are taxed separately from capital results

Both are supported in the exporter with one switch. Daily aggregation is the default: one row per UTC day per asset with the count of hourly payments in the description, so an auditor can trace it. Hourly rows are available if your accountant insists.

Getting the exact numbers

Hyperliquid's public API returns every funding settlement for a wallet with timestamp, coin, rate, position size and USDC delta. The exporter pages through that history for the tax year, sums per day, and prints a per-asset total in summary.txt:

  asset        closes   realized PnL        fees      funding          net
  ETH               1         100.00        2.13        -0.15        97.72
  BTC               1        -100.00        1.81         0.30      -101.51

"Net" is realized PnL minus fees plus funding, the number most accountants start from.

Checking yourself

Open Hyperliquid's portfolio page for the wallet and compare the funding total for the period with the exporter's funding column. Small differences come from UTC day boundaries; a large difference usually means a second wallet or sub-account is involved.
